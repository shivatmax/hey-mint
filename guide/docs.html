Install

One download with everything inside: Python, libraries and models. Apple silicon Macs on macOS 14.2 or later.

  1. Download Hey-Mint-…-arm64.dmg from the latest release and drag Hey Mint into Applications.
  2. Open it. The first time, macOS may say it can't check it: open System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
  3. Paste a free Gemini API key. Hold ⌥ while opening Mint to change keys later.
  4. The welcome window takes it from there: your name and what to call Mint, a voice, the permissions (each with why, and an Allow button), your shortcuts and a tour.

Talking to Mint

Say “Hey Mint”, then ask. Your words and Mint's reply appear as captions beside the orb. Keep talking without the wake word: it's one conversation.

  • what's on my calendar today?
  • and tomorrow?
  • stop
  • bye
ToDo this
Wake it“Hey Mint”, click the orb, or ⌘J and type
InterruptJust talk while it speaks; it stops and listens
Stop everything“stop”, “cancel”, “never mind”: queued clicks and keys too
Send it to sleep“bye”, “that's all”: a one-line goodbye (“Bye, Boss.”), then quiet; or 12 quiet seconds

A lone “hmm” or “okay” gets no answer, unless Mint has just asked you something: it only speaks when there's something to say.

The chat window

The buttons along the top show or hide Mint in screen sharing, compact or clear the chat, send Mint to sleep, turn the mic or spoken replies off, and open Settings. Typing never turns the microphone on.

Reading the orb

The orb turns into whatever it's doing, with a caption, then shows a ✓ or ✗. Long tasks get a plan, a progress ring and confetti.

You seeIt means
Eyes following your pointerAwake and listening
Eyes up, swirl turningThinking

Dimmed, eyes closedAsleep; only “Hey Mint” wakes it

Apps, clicking and typing

Mint reads every control in the window, presses the one you meant like a person would, and checks something changed. Menus and app scripting come first; the pointer moves only when nothing else can do it.

  • open Figma
  • switch to the Jira tab
  • click Sign in
  • type see you at six
  • read this page
  • summarise this
  • in Finder go to Applications
  • scroll to Privacy & Security

Mint never types into a password field, and presses Send, Delete, Buy and the like only when you asked.

Files

Spotlight search by name and content; reads text, PDFs, Word, CSV and more; writes and edits with a backup of the old version.

  • what did I download today?
  • find the PDF about the lease
  • make a file called plan.md with…
  • change the deadline in plan.md to Friday
  • move it to Archive
  • show it in Finder
  • merge acme_old and the Acme copy folder into Acme, keep the newest
  • delete the duplicates in Downloads
  • put the last three things I copied into notes.md
  • what's 1,200.50 plus 85 plus 310.49?

Made files go where you say: “save it in ~/Reports” or “as ~/Shop/cheapest.xlsx” (spreadsheets, PDFs and documents), otherwise into your Mint folder. Asking for a file that already exists adds to it rather than replacing it, and says so. A typo in a file name (“budgte.txt”) is caught and the right file used; “scores.pdf” when only scores.csv exists is found too.

Merging folders keeps the most recently changed copy of each file, puts the older ones in an older folder with their real names and removes the folders left empty. Duplicates means the same contents, never just a similar name: “photo (1).jpg” can be a different photo. Totals and conversions are worked out exactly, not guessed.

“Delete” means the Trash. Keys, keychains, browser profiles, Mail and Messages are off limits.

Browser and web

Full control of Chrome, Safari, Brave, Arc and Edge, including sites you're signed in to. Web search and page reading happen in the background.

  • what tabs do I have open?
  • put my name in the Full name field
  • put YouTube and anime in a group called Entertainment
  • always open YouTube in Brave
  • what's the latest version of Python?
  • read that article
Which browser it uses

The one you name; then your rules (“entertainment in Brave”); then the one in front; then the Mac's default. A tab already showing the page is reused. Page actions are fastest with Allow JavaScript from Apple Events on (Chrome ▸ View ▸ Developer); Mint never switches it on itself.

Shortcuts and dictation

No need to say “Hey Mint”: press a key. And hold a key to dictate anywhere, like Wispr Flow: talk, let go, and your words are typed where your cursor is, cleaned up.

  • ⌃⌥Space Talk to Mint, without the wake word.
  • Hold Right ⌥ Dictate at the cursor, in any app. Tap it twice to dictate hands-free, and once more to finish.
  • ⌘J Open or close the chat.
  • ⌃⌥V Open the clipboard.

Change any of them in Settings ▸ Shortcuts: click one and press the keys you want. Dictation can be one key on its own (Right ⌥, Right ⌘, Right ⌃, Right ⇧ or fn) or a combination. It writes what you meant: punctuation, no “um”s, your corrections applied, lists as lists, Hindi, English or both in the script you'd type. It knows the app you're in, so a chat gets a chat message and Mail gets an email. Add names and jargon under “Words to spell exactly”.

While you dictate, Mint doesn't listen or answer. Your clipboard is put back after the paste. “Paste my last dictation” brings back the last one if it landed nowhere.

The island: Mint changes shape

Like the Dynamic Island, the orb turns into whatever is going on, then springs back. A tiny Mint, still blinking and watching your pointer, stays where the orb was. Click it for the chat.

  • On a call: ● record · 🎙 mic on or off · 🎥 video on or off · ×
  • Recording: the time, bars that move with the call, and Stop
  • Teaching Mint a skill: the time, steps so far, pause, done, cancel
  • Watching a video: the title, keyframes popping in, and what it's doing
  • Your day: a card with the weather, today's timeline, reminders and headlines
  • A lesson, a screen recording: the step and what to do, or “Record this area?”

Answers as cards

When the answer is a number or a short list, the island shows it too: a big count, or rows with icons. A file opens with a click.

  • how many unread emails do I have?
  • what did I download today?
  • what automations do I have?
  • what are you tracking?

While the island shows, Mint's word bubble stays hidden; the words are still in the chat. Like all of Mint, it never appears in screenshots or screen shares.

Drop things on Mint

Drag a file, a few files, a folder or some text towards Mint: a drop target appears. Let go, and Mint offers what fits: summarize, translate into a Word doc, make a spreadsheet, copy the text, watch a video, add captions, transcribe audio. Or just tell it.

The drop target shows only while you're dragging and closes when you let go elsewhere.

Clipboard

Mint keeps everything you copy: text you copy with ⌘C, every screenshot, and whatever Mint copies for you. Press ⌃⌥V or say “open my clipboard” and Mint turns into it, right where the orb sits.

Pick several. Tap the circle on a clip to add it (or ⌘-click; ⇧-click picks a run, ⌘A picks everything). Each circle shows its number, which is the order the clips are pasted in. Then press Paste to paste them into the app you were in, or Copy to paste them yourself. Several pictures or files are copied as files, so one ⌘V attaches them all. Double-click a clip to paste only that one, or drag it out into any app. Pasting doesn't remove anything from the list.

Three tabs. All shows everything. Mint shows what Mint copied and your screenshots. Pinned keeps up to 20 clips for good, like an address, a sign-off or a reply you often use. The search box looks through all of them.

  • open my clipboard
  • paste the last 5 screenshots in the chat
  • copy this and call it the invoice number
  • pin this as my address
  • paste my address
  • save what I copied as my GitHub key
  • copy my GitHub key
  • what did I copy earlier?
  • screenshots to the clipboard only

A screenshot of one window works even when that window is covered, on another Space, or a full-screen app: “screenshot my Claude Code window”, “take a screenshot of the Chrome window and save it in ~/Desktop/shots”. Ask from Telegram and the picture comes to your phone.

Screenshots are numbered, so “paste screenshot 3” works. By default they're saved as a file and copied to the clipboard; you can say “screenshots to the clipboard only” or “as files only” to change that. History stays on this Mac for 7 days (the last 150 clips).

Passwords and keys are never kept in the history. “Save this as my … key” puts it in the macOS Keychain; copying it back clears it from the clipboard after 60 seconds. The clipboard never shows up in screen shares.

Your notifications

Ask what you missed. Mint reads Notification Center, groups it by app and tells you what matters, then opens, clears or answers the one you mean.

  • what did I miss?
  • read my notifications
  • any messages from Slack?
  • open the second one
  • clear the GitHub ones
  • reply to that saying I'm on my way

It uses the Accessibility permission Mint already has, so there's nothing new to allow; Notification Center flashes open for a moment while it reads. Replies are read back to you and only sent when you say so.

Control Mint from your phone (Telegram)

Text your own Telegram bot, or send it a voice note, from anywhere. Mint does it on your Mac and shows its work in the chat as it goes: one message that ticks off each step, then the answer, with screenshots and files it made.

  1. In Telegram, open @BotFather, send /newbot, pick a name and a username ending in “bot”, and copy the token it gives you.
  2. In Mint, open Settings ▸ Accounts & keys ▸ Telegram remote control, add the token and switch it on.
  3. Send your bot the 6-digit code shown there. From then on only your Telegram account can control Mint.
  • what's on my calendar today?
  • find the Q3 report and send it here
  • /screenshot
  • /status
  • /stop

It works like an app inside Telegram. The live message shows a plan, each step as it's done, the time and the app in use, with ⏸ Pause · ⏹ Stop · 📸 Screen buttons; when it finishes, 🔁 Run again · ↩️ Undo. Quick buttons sit under the typing box (What did I miss? · My day · Screen · Stop · Status), and the ☰ menu lists every command: /status, /screenshot, /briefing, /missed, /clipboard (tap one of your recent copies to put it back on the Mac), /trackers, /undo, /chatgpt, /claude, /pause, /resume, /stop, /help. Mint's cards (your schedule, lists) arrive as pictures, and files it made as attachments.

Send it files. Attach a PDF, document, photo, video or audio file (up to 20 MB, Telegram's limit for bots). Add a caption like “summarise this” or “make this a Hindi Word doc” and Mint saves it in your Mint folder's “From phone” and does it; send it without a caption and you get buttons: Summarise, Translate, To spreadsheet, Copy text, Show on Mac.

Get things from the Mac. /clip sends what's on the clipboard, /last your last screenshots, /files what Mint made recently, /send any file or folder (as a zip) by name, and /paste puts text on the Mac's clipboard. Passwords, keys and private folders are never sent.

⏸ Pause stops the current step and holds the plan; ▶️ Resume carries on from where it stopped without redoing finished steps. Alerts from trackers (“the download finished”) and agents arrive with buttons to send the file here or open it on the Mac.

Only your Mac talks to Telegram, over HTTPS: no server, nothing to open on your router. Keep the Mac awake and plugged in; while the screen is locked, clicking and typing in apps may fail, but files, the web, agents and reminders still work. “Read-only from Telegram” stops phone requests from sending, deleting or buying anything. Every phone request is logged on the Mac.

ChatGPT and Claude

Mint can use the ChatGPT and Claude desktop apps for you: ask something, read the answer back, see whether they are still working, and tell you when they finish.

  • ask ChatGPT to outline a talk on local-first apps
  • what did ChatGPT answer?
  • is Claude still working?
  • open my Task auditor session in Claude
  • tell me when Claude is done
  • stop ChatGPT

It checks the prompt landed in the app's message box before pressing Send once, and it only sends when you asked it to. Answers are read to you as quotes: Mint never takes instructions from them. For Claude, Mint works in Claude Code sessions; the Chat view is only read, and only when you ask for it.

Undo

Changed your mind? “Undo that” reverses what Mint just did, and “undo the last three things” goes further back. “What can you undo?” lists them.

  • undo that
  • undo the brightness change
  • undo the last 3 things
  • redo
  • what can you undo?

It covers brightness, volume, Night Shift, Wi-Fi, keep awake, Do Not Disturb, dark mode, window layouts, text Mint rewrote or typed, reminders and notes it made, files it moved, renamed, created or trashed, clipboard changes and settings you changed by voice. The last 50 actions of the past day are kept, on this Mac. Some things can't be taken back, like a message that was sent, and Mint says so.

Everyday and long tasks

Calendar, reminders, notes, timers, email drafts, volume and media. Ask for a list of things and Mint does them back to back, then reports once.

  • remind me to call Sam at five
  • set a ten minute timer for tea
  • draft an email to Priya saying I'll be late
  • take a screenshot of this window
  • open Notes, Calendar and Slack
  • what did I copy before?

Bigger jobs get a plan you can watch step by step. Mint checks each step really happened, splits a step that turns out bigger, and keeps notes as it goes. The plan is saved, so “stop” only pauses it: say where were we? tomorrow and it carries on.

Emails are always drafts: Mint never sends one. A message goes out only when you asked, and never twice within two minutes.

Watching videos

Give Mint a link, a file or “this video” on screen. In seconds it tells you what the video is, the key moments with timestamps, and how it looks and feels.

  • watch this and tell me what it's about
  • what did he say about pricing?
  • show me the frame at 3:20
  • what's the aesthetic of this reel?
  • transcribe my screen recording

Mint doesn't sit through the video. It reads what's said, from the captions or by transcribing the audio, and looks at about a dozen frames picked where the scene changes. A 15-minute talk takes about 15 seconds. The transcript and notes are saved in Documents ▸ Mint ▸ videos.

Works with YouTube, X, Vimeo, Loom, TikTok and Instagram links, and any video or audio file on your Mac. Agents can watch videos for their research too.

Meeting notes

When a call starts, the orb turns into a recorder. Press ● and Mint quietly records the call. No bot joins. At the end you get a transcript and notes.

  • take notes of this meeting
  • record this meeting with video
  • what's been said so far?
  • the meeting is over
  • what did we decide in the Acme call?
  • open yesterday's meeting notes

Mint records the call's sound and your microphone separately, so the notes know what you said and what everyone else said. They have a summary, decisions, action items with owners, open questions and key quotes, all with timestamps. They're saved in your Mint folder ▸ Meetings. It works with Google Meet, Zoom, Teams, Slack huddles and FaceTime.

Turn on 🎥 and Mint also records a video of the call's window, with sound, into the same meeting folder. Turn off 🎙 and only the call is recorded. Asking for a summary in the middle doesn't stop the recording: you get the notes so far, and it carries on.

The first time, macOS asks to let Mint record system audio. Mint records only after you press record. When a call starts it offers, and you can turn that off in Settings ▸ Storage & Privacy.

Documents: copy, spreadsheet, translate

Copy the text off anything on screen, turn a table in a picture or PDF into a real Excel file, or turn an English PDF into a Hindi Word document, with its headings, lists and tables kept.

  • OCR this and copy the text
  • copy the text from this image
  • make an Excel of this table
  • put the data in this PDF into a spreadsheet
  • convert this English PDF to a Hindi Word doc
  • translate report.docx to Spanish as a PDF
  • turn this PDF into a Word document

Text is read on your Mac (scanned PDFs too) and copied as it reads, tables with tabs. Spreadsheets get a bold, frozen header, filters and real numbers and dates, and phone numbers stay as text. Translations keep the document's shape and are saved next to the original, like “report (Hindi).docx”. Long documents carry on in the background, and Mint tells you when they're done.

Pictures: make and edit

Describe a picture and Apple's Image Playground draws it on your Mac, in seconds. It opens on a card by the orb: type or say a change and it redraws, with every version kept to go back to.

  • make an illustration of a lighthouse at sunset
  • draw a fox in the snow as a sketch
  • make the sky purple
  • make another one
  • improve it
  • save it to my Desktop
  • copy it
  • open it in Image Playground

The card has Save…, Another (a new variation), Improve (a richer description, drawn again), Copy and Playground (for Visual Edit and captions in Apple's app). Drag the picture out into any app. Pictures are kept in your Mint folder ▸ Images.

Needs Apple Intelligence (Image Playground). The first time, Mint opens two small shortcuts, “Mint Draw” and “Mint Redraw”: click Add Shortcut on each. Everything is made on your Mac.

Editing videos by voice

Say what to change: Mint plans the edit, renders it next to the original (never over it) and checks the result before telling you.

  • cut the first 10 seconds
  • trim it to 0:30 to 1:45
  • make it vertical for Reels
  • add captions
  • remove the silent parts
  • compress it under 25 MB
  • make a GIF of 0:10 to 0:15
  • join these clips
  • add this music quietly underneath

It works on the video you just edited, the one selected in Finder, or the newest one, and the island shows the progress. Every edit is checked: the length, the size, the sound, and the captions on a few frames. Needs ffmpeg (brew install ffmpeg).

Teach it, or learn from it

Show Mint a task once and it can do it from then on. Or ask Mint to show you, and it points at each button while you do it yourself.

  • watch me do this once
  • pause, don't record this
  • carry on
  • done, that's how
  • show me how to export a PDF in Preview
  • next
  • stop the lesson

While Mint watches, your pointer gets a ring in Mint's colour, and each click snaps a little frame and shows its step number, so it's clear what's being recorded. Pause it from the island, and nothing is recorded until you carry on. Clicks on Mint itself are never part of the skill.

While it watches, Mint notes which buttons you click and what you type. It never records passwords, and it stops by itself after 10 minutes. The first time, macOS asks for Input Monitoring.

Briefings, email, translation and more

A one-minute spoken rundown of your day. Your Apple Shortcuts by voice. Any screenshot, found by what's in it.

Reminders, notes and events go where you say: “on my Work calendar”, “in my Groceries list”, “in the Recipes folder”. A clash is spotted before booking (“use the next free half hour if it clashes”). Email drafts open in the Mail app when you say so.

  • good morning, brief me
  • every weekday at 8:30, give me my daily briefing
  • turn on the living room lights
  • run my Good Night shortcut
  • find the screenshot with the invoice number
  • add my action items from that meeting to Reminders
  • translate this page
  • triage my email
  • draft a reply to the second one saying I'll send it Friday

The briefing covers your calendar, reminders due, which emails need you, unfinished tasks, the weather and a few headlines. For lights and scenes, save them as shortcuts in the Shortcuts app and Mint runs them. The text in your screenshots is read on your Mac. Foreign text on screen is translated in place: each paragraph is covered in its own background and the translation is written over it, in a colour and size that fit, for a minute (“clear the marks” takes it away). Email replies are written the way you write, and they stay drafts until you send them.

Let me know when it's done

Ask Mint to keep an eye on something and carry on with your day. The moment it finishes, Mint says so, shows a notification, and the island shows what finished. Click it to open.

  • let me know when this download finishes
  • tell me when the Task auditor Claude session is done
  • ping me when the build finishes
  • let me know when the upload is complete
  • tell me when report.pdf has finished exporting
  • what are you tracking?
  • stop tracking the download
  • Downloads (Chrome, Brave, Edge, Arc, Safari, Firefox): the browser's partial file in Downloads; its size and speed show the progress.
  • Claude Code sessions (the terminal and the desktop app's Code tab): the session's own log. Mint also tells you when a session is waiting for your approval.
  • Commands in Terminal or iTerm: done when nothing but the shell is left running in that tab; you hear how it ended.
  • Anything in a window (an upload, a render, an export, an AI chat): progress bars and the window's text, and every so often Gemini checks a small picture of the window against what you asked for.
  • A file: it exists and has stopped growing.

Trackers keep running for up to 12 hours and survive a restart. Saying “stop” doesn't end them; “stop tracking …” does. The first time Mint watches a Terminal or iTerm tab, macOS asks to let Mint control it.

Things Mint does by itself: at a time, every so often, or when something happens.

Memory

Mint remembers facts about you, one per note, and looks up only what matters to the question.

  • remember my manager is Meera
  • my manager is now Rahul
  • who's my boss?
  • forget my old address
  • what do you remember about me?

When a fact changes, Mint replaces it and still remembers what it used to be. Once a day it tidies up: it merges duplicates and drops things whose date has passed. Read and edit every fact in the Skills & Memory window. Passwords, keys and card numbers are refused.

What happened when

Mint can look back through your conversations, what it did, what the agents found, and the videos it watched, by day.

  • what did we do yesterday?
  • when did I ask you about flights?
  • what was that site you found last week?
  • what did Astra find on Monday?

Say remember what I work on to turn on the activity timeline. It notes which app, window and web page is in front. Then you can ask what was I working on yesterday afternoon?, find that Airbnb tab from Tuesday or how long was I in Slack today?.

The timeline is off until you turn it on. It is text only, never screenshots or typing, and skips private windows and password managers. Safari pages are never recorded, because Safari can't tell its private windows apart. It stays on your Mac for 14 days. Say delete my timeline to erase it.

Skills

A skill is a short how-to. When a task took many steps or needed your correction, Mint writes it up, then ranks it by how often it works.

  • save how to do that as a skill
  • next time use the sidebar
  • what skills do you have?
  • delete that skill

Wake word and voice lock

“Hey Mint” is detected on your Mac. Train your voice once (menu bar ▸ Train my voice…, two minutes) and only you can wake it, talk to it, or talk over it.

SettingUse it when

StrictSomeone with a similar voice is around
  • you heard cube con, I said KubeCon
  • it's GitHub, one word

Mint steps aside during Zoom, Meet or FaceTime calls, and ignores talk that isn't meant for it.

Your own wake word. Want “Hey Jarvis” or “Okay Nova”? Type it in Settings ▸ Voice & wake word and press Train. Mint builds a detector for it on your Mac in about a minute and a half, from dozens of voices plus (if you like) four takes of your own voice. In testing, “Hey Jarvis” caught 93% of voices it had never heard, with about one false wake an hour of other people talking. Keep “Hey Mint” as well if you like, press Test to try it, or Reset to go back. If the voice lock is on, retrain your voice after changing the phrase.

Apple Shortcuts, made for you

Mint runs your Shortcuts by voice, and makes new ones from plain words: say what it should do, hear the steps, say yes, and it opens in the Shortcuts app for one “Add Shortcut” click.

  • make me a shortcut that turns on dark mode and sets the volume to 30%
  • make a shortcut that tells me the weather and my battery
  • run Hello and Date
  • what shortcuts do I have?
  • add Mint's Focus shortcuts

Settings ▸ Apple Shortcuts lists Mint's shortcuts (the ones it uses for Image Playground and Do Not Disturb, with an Add button for any that are missing), your shortcuts (each with Run and a “Mint may run it” switch), and Create a shortcut: type what it should do, Plan it, then Create.

Mint builds from 39 Shortcuts actions whose exact format it has checked: speaking and showing text, notifications, the clipboard, apps and web pages, volume, brightness, dark mode, Focus, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, weather, battery, the date, dictation, calculations, Apple Intelligence, music playlists, calendar events, reminders, email drafts, Image Playground pictures, menus and repeats. If something isn't possible it says so rather than guessing.

Mint never makes a shortcut that sends messages or deletes things. An email is only ever a draft, a shell command is always shown in the plan first, and by voice nothing is made until you say yes.

Language. By default Mint answers in the language you speak. Pick one in Speaking ▸ Language (Hindi, Spanish, Japanese and 20 more) and it always speaks and writes in it.

Updates

Hey Mint updates itself. When a new version is out, it downloads in the background and installs while you're away from the Mac, replacing the app in place and reopening it. No second copy, and your permissions stay.

Each update is checked before it is installed: its checksum must match the release, and it must be signed with the same certificate as the app you have. Anything else is refused. Turn it off, or check by hand, in Settings ▸ Updates & Help. The first version with updates (0.3.0) is installed by hand once; after that it's automatic.

Report a problem

Something went wrong? Choose Report a problem… in the menu bar (or Settings ▸ Updates & Help). A new GitHub issue opens in your browser with your version and macOS filled in.

The recent errors from Mint's log are copied to your clipboard, with paths, emails, keys and everything you or Mint said removed, so you can paste them in if you like. Nothing is sent until you submit the issue yourself.

Privacy and safety

🎙️Nothing leaves your Mac while Mint is asleep. The wake word and voice lock run on the Mac.
🔒Never enters passwords or card numbers, and never saves secrets in files, skills or memory.
✉️Never sends email; messages and forms go out only when you asked.
🗂️“Delete” means the Trash; files are backed up before they're changed; private folders are off limits.
✋“Stop” halts everything at once, including queued clicks and keys.
