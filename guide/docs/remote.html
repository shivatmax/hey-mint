From anywhere

Use your Mac from anywhere: your own Telegram bot, a Google Meet that shares your screen, or plain email.

Control Mint from your phone (Telegram)

Text your own Telegram bot, or send it a voice note, from anywhere. Mint does it on your Mac and shows its work in the chat as it goes: one message that ticks off each step, then the answer, with screenshots and files it made.

  1. In Telegram, open @BotFather, send /newbot, pick a name and a username ending in “bot”, and copy the token it gives you.
  2. In Mint, open Settings ▸ Accounts & keys ▸ Telegram remote control, add the token and switch it on.
  3. Send your bot the 6-digit code shown there. From then on only your Telegram account can control Mint.
  • what's on my calendar today?
  • find the Q3 report and send it here
  • /screenshot
  • /status
  • /stop

It works like an app inside Telegram. The live message shows a plan, each step as it's done, the time and the app in use, with ⏸ Pause · ⏹ Stop · 📸 Screen buttons; when it finishes, 🔁 Run again · ↩️ Undo. Quick buttons sit under the typing box (What did I miss? · My day · Screen · Stop · Status), and the ☰ menu lists every command: /status, /screenshot, /briefing, /missed, /clipboard (tap one of your recent copies to put it back on the Mac), /trackers, /undo, /chatgpt, /claude, /pause, /resume, /stop, /meet (a Google Meet with Mint), /help. Mint's cards (your schedule, lists) arrive as pictures, and files it made as attachments.

Send it files. Attach a PDF, document, photo, video or audio file (up to 20 MB, Telegram's limit for bots). Add a caption like “summarise this” or “make this a Hindi Word doc” and Mint saves it in your Mint folder's “From phone” and does it; send it without a caption and you get buttons: Summarise, Translate, To spreadsheet, Copy text, Show on Mac.

Get things from the Mac. /clip sends what's on the clipboard, /last your last screenshots, /files what Mint made recently, /send any file or folder (as a zip) by name, and /paste puts text on the Mac's clipboard. Passwords, keys and private folders are never sent.

⏸ Pause stops the current step and holds the plan; ▶️ Resume carries on from where it stopped without redoing finished steps. Alerts from trackers (“the download finished”) and agents arrive with buttons to send the file here or open it on the Mac.

Only your Mac talks to Telegram, over HTTPS: no server, nothing to open on your router. Keep the Mac awake and plugged in; while the screen is locked, clicking and typing in apps may fail, but files, the web, agents and reminders still work. “Read-only from Telegram” stops phone requests from sending, deleting or buying anything. Every phone request is logged on the Mac.

Google Meet with Mint: your Mac, from anywhere

Say “start a Google Meet”, send /meet on Telegram, or email it. Mint makes a meeting, joins it, shares your Mac's screen and sends you the link. Join from your phone and talk to Mint like on a phone call while you watch it work, or type in the call's chat.

  • start a Google Meet
  • stop sharing
  • share the screen again
  • is the call on?
  • end the call

The first time: Mint's Meet window is its own Chrome profile. When you start the first call, Google's sign-in opens there: Mint fills in the address, you type the password (Mint never does) and approve on your phone. After that every call starts in a few seconds. A separate Google account just for Mint works well.

In the call: the call is Mint's microphone and speaker - the Mac stays silent, the wake word isn't needed, and Mint doesn't fall asleep. Whatever you say is for Mint; a half-finished sentence gets no answer until you finish it. Messages in the call's chat are requests too, and Mint answers there as well as out loud.

Who gets in: whoever has the link and asks to join is let in at once, and Telegram tells you who (Settings ▸ Voice ▸ Google Meet calls, or meet_admit: everyone / first / ask). Meet itself turns away people who aren't signed in to a Google account. Mint leaves 3 minutes after everyone has gone, if nobody comes in 10 minutes, or after 2 hours.

Everything runs on your Mac: Chrome, the free Google Meet, no server and no audio driver. While a call is on, Mint keeps the Mac awake. It can't start a call on a locked Mac (it never types your password) - if you leave the Mac at home to use it from afar, keep it plugged in and decide yourself whether it should lock when the display sleeps.

Control Mint by email

No Telegram? Email Mint. Send a mail whose subject starts with Mint: to the address Mint watches - it does it and answers in the same thread, as a formatted email with the steps it took, files and screenshots attached, and a Join button when it started a call.

  1. Use a Gmail address for Mint (a separate one is best). Turn on 2-Step Verification there, then create an app password at myaccount.google.com/apppasswords.
  2. In Mint, open Settings ▸ Accounts & keys ▸ Email control: the address, the app password (kept in the Keychain), who may send, and switch it on.
  3. Email it: Mint: what's on my calendar today? Commands work too: /screenshot, /status, /stop, /meet, /meet end, /briefing, /missed, /help.

Fast: Mint holds the mailbox open (IMAP IDLE), so a new mail reaches it in a second or two, and the reply goes out as soon as Mint is done. Requests run one at a time, each with its whole answer. Without an app password it can use Apple Mail instead (slower: Mail's own fetching).

Safe: a mail is only acted on when the subject has the prefix, the sender is on your list (or, if you allow anyone, the mail carries your secret word), and Google's own check proves the sender is real - a faked From address is ignored. Old mail, duplicates, mailing lists and auto-replies are skipped, and at most 20 requests an hour run. Replies go only to that sender. When Mint wants to delete or change something, it emails the question and waits for your YES or NO. Every other mail tool stays drafts-only.
