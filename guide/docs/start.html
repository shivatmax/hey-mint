Get started

Install Hey Mint, talk to it, read the orb, try notch mode, and learn the shortcuts and dictation.

Install

One download with everything inside: Python, libraries and models. Apple silicon Macs on macOS 14.2 or later.

Download it (no Terminal)

  1. Download Hey-Mint-…-arm64.dmg from the latest release, open it and drag Hey Mint onto Applications. (Opened straight from the DMG or Downloads, Hey Mint offers to move itself there: click Move to Applications.)
  2. Open Hey Mint from Applications. The first time, macOS says “Apple could not verify ‘Hey Mint’ is free of malware…”: click Done.
  3. Open System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security, scroll down to “Hey Mint” was blocked, click Open Anyway, type your Mac password and click Open Anyway again. (On macOS 14: right-click Hey Mint ▸ Open ▸ Open.)

Once per Mac, never again. New versions install themselves, without that question and keeping Mint's permissions, so there's no need to download the DMG again. Why macOS asks at all: Hey Mint is free and open source, and Apple only skips this question for developers who pay it a yearly fee.

Or one line in Terminal (macOS asks nothing)

Open Terminal (Spotlight ▸ “Terminal”), paste this and press Return:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/shivatmax/hey-mint/main/packaging/install.sh | bash

It downloads the latest Hey Mint, checks it, puts it in Applications, approves it with macOS (the same thing Open Anyway does) and opens it. It is a short script you can read first; it's also at hey-mint.pages.dev/install.sh.

Then

  1. The welcome window takes it from there: your name, what to call Mint and where it lives (in the notch or floating), then Connect: open Google AI Studio, click Create API key (free), and press Paste. Mint checks the key with Google before saving it on this Mac. The next page is optional: a Jev key for faster, surer clicking - open TypeSafe's console, create a key, press Paste (or Skip; without Jev, Gemini makes those choices).
  2. Then a voice, the permissions (each with why, and an Allow button), your shortcuts and a short tour. Change the key any time in Settings ▸ Models & agents.

Build from source instead 
git clone https://github.com/shivatmax/hey-mint.git && cd hey-mint
./set-key.sh      # your Gemini key, saved in .env
./install.sh      # builds ~/Applications/Mint.app
open ~/Applications/Mint.app

Talking to Mint

Say “Hey Mint”, then ask. Your words and Mint's reply appear as captions beside the orb. Right after it answers you can follow up without the wake word for a few seconds; then it goes back to waiting for “Hey Mint”.

  • what's on my calendar today?
  • and tomorrow?
  • stop
  • bye
ToDo this
Wake it“Hey Mint”, click the orb, or ⌘J and type
InterruptJust talk while it speaks; it stops and listens
Stop everything“stop”, “cancel”, “never mind”: queued clicks and keys too
Send it to sleep“bye”, “that's all”: a one-line goodbye (“Bye, Boss.”), then quiet; or just stay quiet for 6 seconds after a reply

Only what's meant for Mint. Before Mint answers or does anything with what it heard without the wake word, it checks the words are for it and ask it something: talk with someone else, a video or call in the background, a passing phrase get no reply and no action, and don't keep it listening. A lone “hmm” or “okay” gets no answer either, unless Mint has just asked you something. “Stop”, and yes or no to the guard, always get through. Settings ▸ General ▸ Keep listening after a reply: 4 to 12 seconds, or never (always say “Hey Mint”).

If Mint ever freezes. Mint watches itself: if it stops responding, a ⚠ appears in the menu bar with Restart Mint, and after about 40 seconds it restarts by itself, writing where it got stuck to the log. ⌃⌥⌘M restarts it any time, and Settings ▸ General has Restart Mint too. No need to restart the Mac.

If the voice service is down. The caption says what's wrong in a line - no quota left, Google's side, no network, the key - and counts down to the next try, instead of going quiet. If you were waiting for an answer, Mint says so out loud.

Always the quickest voice. Mint can talk through several Gemini voice models, each with its own allowance per minute. If the one in use is slow, stalls, or nears its limit, Mint moves to the next at a quiet moment - or at once, asking it again, when you're left waiting - and comes back when the first is quick again. A model that keeps misbehaving rests longer each time, from minutes to a week. Newer models are picked up on their own.

Light to start. Each conversation starts with Mint's everyday tools only. The rarer ones - video editing, spreadsheets, translating documents, pictures, screen recording and more - are looked up the moment you ask, which adds about a second. If Mint catches itself repeating the same step with nothing changing, it stops and tries another way or asks you.

The chat window

The buttons along the top show or hide Mint in screen sharing, compact or clear the chat, send Mint to sleep, turn the mic or spoken replies off, and open Settings. Typing never turns the microphone on.

Reading the orb

The orb turns into whatever it's doing, with a caption, then shows a ✓ or ✗. Long tasks get a plan, a progress ring and confetti.

You seeIt means
Eyes following your pointerAwake and listening
Eyes up, swirl turningThinking
A symbol with a captionWorking: a magnifier is searching, a hand is clicking, a cursor is typing
Dimmed, eyes closedAsleep; only “Hey Mint” wakes it

Notch mode

On a MacBook with a camera notch, Mint can live in the notch like a Dynamic Island instead of floating as an orb. Say become the notch (or go into the notch), or turn on “Dynamic Island at the notch” in the menu or in Settings ▸ Appearance & Sound. go out of my notch or back to the orb switches back. The switch plays right away, no restart.

You seeIt means
The little Mint left of the cameraMint's real face, only smaller: blinking, watching your pointer, every expression
Sound bars on the rightListening, or speaking when they're mint green
A spinner or a task iconThinking, or working on a task (the notch drops down with the step and a progress bar)
A moon, or a red crossed-out micAsleep, or the microphone is off

It stays compact: it drops down only for real words, a task or progress, and only as wide as the sentence needs. A bare status like “Mic off” stays a small icon.

Hover the notch for a moment and a small row of controls drops down (mic, voice, chat, sleep, screen sharing, hide, menu - and ■ Stop first while Mint is talking or working, the same as saying “stop”). Hide (the arrow into the top bar, the menu's Hide Mint, ⌃⌥H or “hide yourself”) takes the little Mint and the icons beside the camera away - the notch still opens when you point at it or tap it. It comes back by itself when you say “Hey Mint” or it has something to tell you, or with the Show button or ⌃⌥H (orb mode: the orb fades out the same way). Hovering never opens more than that, so crossing the notch on your way somewhere does nothing. Click the notch (anywhere but a button) and the full notch opens; click it again and it folds back (left alone for a few seconds, it folds by itself). The chat has its own button and opens right under the notch; click the notch to close it again. Drag the little Mint away from the notch: it stretches like it is held by an elastic, springs back if you let go early, and if you pull it out, it leaves the notch and Mint becomes the floating orb. Right-click for the menu. Whatever opens - the chat, the clipboard, a picture, a calendar card, the meeting recorder, a lesson - doesn't float below the notch: the notch grows around it and it turns notch-black, so the notch itself becomes it. Two at once stack instead of overlapping. On a screen without a notch, the same shape hangs from the middle of the menu bar.

It grows for what matters, then folds back. Files Mint finds open the notch as tiles at once, even while it is still answering; a song that starts shows the player for a few seconds; hovering always opens the whole notch, with Mint's words inside if it is talking. When you say “Hey Mint” while music plays, the little Mint takes the notch back from the album art so you can see it is listening, and a paused song gives the notch back after a few seconds.

Home and Shelf. Hover the notch and it opens into a small home: the song that's playing (or Mint), your calendar - a strip of days with today's remaining events, click a day to see its events, click an event to open it in Calendar - and your battery. The Shelf tab holds files for later: drag a file onto the notch and it opens on the Shelf, ready to take it. From there drag files back out into any app, double-click to open, right-click to share, AirDrop, show in Finder or remove, or press AirDrop to send them. The shelf only remembers where your files are; it never moves or copies them, and it follows a file you rename or move.

Search in the notch. “Find my launch files”, “what did I download today?”: the results open in the notch as tiles with previews. Hover one to see where it is (its folder under the name; the full path in the bigger grid); double-click opens it, Space previews it, drag it out into any app, and the arrows button grows the notch into a 3 × 1, 3 × 2 or 3 × 3 grid. Type in the search field to look for something else, or press Ask Mint.

Charging and music. Plug in or unplug the charger and the notch says so for a moment next to the battery (green while charging, red when low). When a song starts, the notch becomes the player for a few seconds - cover, time and controls - then shrinks to the cover and dancing bars.

Settings ▸ Appearance & Sound ▸ Notch: words in the notch, controls on hover, the little Mint when idle (off: a plain notch until something happens) and playful motion (bouncy springs, a hop as it opens, breathing with Mint's voice).

Shortcuts and dictation

No need to say “Hey Mint”: press a key. And hold a key to dictate anywhere, like Wispr Flow: talk, let go, and your words are typed where your cursor is, cleaned up.

  • ⌃⌥Space Talk to Mint, without the wake word.
  • Hold Right ⌥ Dictate at the cursor, in any app. Tap it twice to dictate hands-free, and once more to finish.
  • ⌘J Open or close the chat.
  • ⌃⌥V Open the clipboard.

Change any of them in Settings ▸ Shortcuts: click one and press the keys you want. Dictation can be one key on its own (Right ⌥, Right ⌘, Right ⌃, Right ⇧ or fn) or a combination. It writes what you meant: punctuation, no “um”s, your corrections applied, lists as lists, Hindi, English or both in the script you'd type. It knows the app you're in, so a chat gets a chat message and Mail gets an email. Add names and jargon under “Words to spell exactly”.

While you dictate, Mint doesn't listen or answer. Your clipboard is put back after the paste. “Paste my last dictation” brings back the last one if it landed nowhere.
