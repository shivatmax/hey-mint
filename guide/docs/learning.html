Memory and learning

What Mint remembers about you, the journal of what happened when, the skills it learns, and teaching it a task by showing it once.

Memory

Mint remembers what you tell it and what you do together, keeps the important things in every conversation, and finds the rest by meaning when it matters.

  • remember my manager is Meera
  • my manager is now Rahul
  • who's my boss?
  • forget my old address
  • what do you remember about me?

Three kinds of memory. About you: how you like things done (“keep replies short”, “open apps full screen”). Facts: people, work, accounts, places. Journal: what happened on a day, written as you go. What you told Mint always counts more than what it worked out by itself.

Always at hand. Every conversation starts with About you and the facts that matter most - ranked by how often they were confirmed and used, never cut off mid-line - plus the last few days. Everything else is found the moment it's needed, by words and by meaning (“that pizza place” finds the note about Luigi's on 5th), with typed and phone requests carrying what's relevant to them. Facts older than three months are marked as possibly out of date.

Kept clean. Saying the same thing twice confirms it instead of saving a copy. When a fact changes, the new one takes over and the old one is kept as history. Mint saves only lasting things - not prices, battery levels or what it clicked. Once a day it merges duplicates, retires facts nobody used in three months (still findable if you ask) and keeps the summary of what's in progress honest. Conversations are saved as they happen, so a crash or restart loses nothing. Read and edit every fact in the Skills & Memory window; forget retires a fact, and forgets it for good when you say so. Passwords, keys and card numbers are refused.

What happened when

Mint can look back through your conversations, what it did, what the agents found, and the videos it watched, by day.

  • what did we do yesterday?
  • when did I ask you about flights?
  • what was that site you found last week?
  • what did Astra find on Monday?

Fast. Mint keeps a private search index of your conversations on your Mac, so what did I ask you about the VS Code extension? is answered in a second or two from just the matching moments, and Mint can read that conversation word for word when it needs to. It looks things up before asking you to repeat yourself.

Long conversations keep your instructions. When a conversation nears the voice model's limit, Mint condenses it at a quiet moment instead of letting the oldest part fall away. Your rules and requests are kept in your own words, along with the files, apps and links involved and any task still in progress. Background jobs carry on through it.

Say remember what I work on to turn on the activity timeline. It notes which app, window and web page is in front. Then you can ask what was I working on yesterday afternoon?, find that Airbnb tab from Tuesday or how long was I in Slack today?.

The timeline is off until you turn it on. It is text only, never screenshots or typing, and skips private windows and password managers. Safari pages are never recorded, because Safari can't tell its private windows apart. It stays on your Mac for 14 days. Say delete my timeline to erase it.

Skills

A skill is a short how-to. When a task took many steps or needed your correction, Mint writes it up, then ranks it by how often it works.

  • save how to do that as a skill
  • next time use the sidebar
  • what skills do you have?
  • delete that skill
  • learn this page
  • what did you learn recently?
  • undo what you learned about installing extensions

Learns after the task. When a task took many steps, went wrong and recovered, or you corrected Mint, it reviews what happened once it has answered - never while you talk. It fixes the skill it used before making a new one, writes rules (“click Install inside the extension's own row - the top button installs the wrong one”) instead of stories, and skips one-offs and glitches. It also notes whether the skills it followed worked.

Your skills stay yours. Mint's learning only rewrites skills Mint made. A skill you wrote, a starter skill or one you taught gets a “Suggested” note at most, and a pinned skill is never touched.

Every change can be undone. Mint records each change to a skill - who, when, why, and the text before and after. Say undo what you learned about…, or press Undo change in Skills & Memory. Skills nobody used for a month sink to the bottom; old ones Mint made itself are archived after three months and can be brought back.

Learn from a page. learn this on a web page, the front window, the clipboard or our conversation turns it into a skill. Every conversation sees the full list of skills, and background jobs use them too.

Teach it, or learn from it

Show Mint a task once and it can do it from then on. Or ask Mint to show you, and it points at each button while you do it yourself.

  • watch me do this once
  • pause, don't record this
  • carry on
  • done, that's how
  • show me how to export a PDF in Preview
  • next
  • stop the lesson

While Mint watches, your pointer gets a ring in Mint's colour, and each click snaps a little frame and shows its step number, so it's clear what's being recorded. Pause it from the island, and nothing is recorded until you carry on. Clicks on Mint itself are never part of the skill.

While it watches, Mint notes which buttons you click and what you type. It never records passwords, and it stops by itself after 10 minutes. The first time, macOS asks for Input Monitoring.
