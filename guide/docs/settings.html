Settings and updates

Every page of Settings, and how Hey Mint updates itself safely.

Open Settings from the menu bar icon (⌘,), or just ask Mint to change something. Pages sit in a sidebar, like System Settings.

Language. By default Mint answers in the language you speak. Pick one in Speaking ▸ Language (Hindi, Spanish, Japanese and 20 more) and it always speaks and writes in it.

Usage. Every request is counted per model: requests, input and output tokens. Only counts, never what was said. Free Gemini keys cost nothing; they have daily limits, and this page shows how close you are.

Google. Add your Google account in System Settings ▸ Internet Accounts (Accounts & keys has a button). Gmail lands in Mail and your Google calendars in Calendar, and Mint works with both on your Mac, with no sign-in to Mint and no server in between.

Mint is invisible in screen shares by default; “be visible” shows it in a Google Meet or Zoom share.

Appearance & Sound

The few things most people change, on one short page. Everything else waits behind More options.

  • View: where Mint lives (a floating orb or the notch), the theme, where the orb sits, and how much it moves. To hide Mint, use the menu bar or ⌃⌥H: it comes out for “Hey Mint” and hides again when it goes back to sleep.
  • Sounds: the little sounds - off until you turn them on - and their volume.
  • Claude Code and Codex: pop up when one asks you something, pop up when one finishes, and how much of your usage limits is used. See Claude mode.
  • More options: the face on the orb and in the menu bar, what the open notch shows first, each effect on its own switch (expressions, the status badge, action flourishes, critter helpers, on-screen effects, the glow round its window, poke to play, the morning hello, little trips), which sounds play, the mini music player, the notch's parts and the rest of Claude mode (Live, the agents bar, Telegram, checks, hooks).

How much it moves

AnimationWhat you get
Full (playful)Everything: bouncy springs, hops, sparkles, idle tricks
CalmGentle: things settle without bouncing, idle tricks are rarer and quieter (a wink or a glance), no little trips or confetti; hearts and notes fade in place instead of floating up
Minimal (fades only)Short fades instead of movement: no travel, bounce or particles; a poke gets a blink

When Reduce motion is on in macOS (System Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Display), Mint always behaves as Minimal, whatever this says.

The little sounds

Mint makes its own soft, short sounds - no sound files, just a few tones made on your Mac.

GroupWhen you hear it
Notch opening and closingThe notch opens or folds (also when you press Esc on it)
TasksA coding agent finishes or fails, an error card, a long job reaching 100%, a request sent from the notch, a file dropped, a tap on Retry or on an agent's chip
PlayPokes, getting dizzy, and the morning hello

Never in the way. Sounds stay quiet while you dictate, while Mint listens to you, while you're on a call (a call app has the mic, or a Google Meet with Mint is on) and while a meeting is being recorded, so a chime never lands in your microphone or a recording. Only the notch opening and closing and the morning hello play while Mint listens.

Updates

Hey Mint updates itself. When a new version is out, it downloads in the background and installs while you're away from the Mac, replacing the app in place and reopening it. No second copy, and your permissions stay.

Each update is checked before it is installed: its checksum must match the release, and it must be signed with the same certificate as the app you have. Anything else is refused. Turn it off, or check by hand, in Settings ▸ Updates & Help. The first version with updates (0.3.0) is installed by hand once; after that it's automatic.
